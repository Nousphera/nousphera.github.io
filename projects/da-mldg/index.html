On efficacy of Meta-Learning for Domain Generalization in Speech Emotion Recognition

Why emotion models struggle beyond their training domain

Raeshak King Gandhi · Vasileios Tsouvalas · Nirvana Meratnia

The idea

At a glance

Speech emotion models often lose accuracy when speakers, languages, or recording datasets change. DA-MLDG studies whether meta-learning can improve that transfer by weighting training episodes according to estimated domain shift; its evaluation exposes the limits of log-mel representations and motivates stronger features and more varied speech emotion datasets.

  • Domain generalization
  • Meta-learning
  • Speech emotion recognition
Read the abstract

Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) refers to the recognition of human emotions from natural speech, vital for building human-centered context-aware intelligent systems. Here, domain shift, where models’ trained on one domain exhibit performance degradation when exposed to an unseen domain with different statistics, is a major limiting factor in SER applicability, as models have a strong dependence on speakers and languages characteristics used during training. Meta-Learning for Domain Generalization (MLDG) has shown great success in improving models’ generalization capacity and alleviate the domain shift problem in the vision domain; yet, its’ efficacy on SER remains largely unexplored. In this work, we propose a “domain-shift aware” MLDG approach to learn generalizable models across multiple domains in SER. Based on our extensive evaluation, we identify a number of pitfalls that contribute to poor models’ DG ability, and demonstrate that log-mel spectrograms representations lack distinct features required for MLDG in SER. We further explore the use of appropriate features to achieve DG in SER as to provide insides to future research directions for DG in SER.

The approach

How it works

  1. 01

    Simulate unseen domains

    Split source-domain batches into non-overlapping meta-train and meta-test sets. Take a training step on the meta-train set before evaluating transfer to the held-out source domains.

  2. 02

    Estimate the domain shift

    Compute the difference between average energy scores on the two sets after the meta-train step. This margin acts as an adaptive measure of the shift between domains.

  3. 03

    Adapt the meta-objective

    Weight the meta-test loss by the estimated shift and optimize it together with the meta-train loss. Evaluate transfer to target corpora that were unavailable during training.

From the paper

Key findings

Modest gains, a persistent gap

Across seven scenarios drawn from ten speech emotion datasets, domain-aware meta-learning gives modest improvements but does not solve generalization. The authors conclude that the resulting performance remains insufficient for practical use.

Representation matters

Replacing log-mel inputs with pre-trained TRILLsson embeddings improves results in several settings, including the supervised baseline. The study points to richer input representations and more varied in-the-wild data as priorities.
