Privacy-preserving Speech Emotion Recognition through Semi-Supervised Federated Learning

Learn speech emotions from audio kept on each device

Vasileios Tsouvalas · Tanir Ozcelebi · Nirvana Meratnia

The idea

At a glance

Speech emotion recognition depends on sensitive audio that is costly to annotate. This work combines an attention-based speech model with federated self-training, using labeled clips and confident predictions on unlabeled clips while keeping raw audio on each device, then studies label scarcity and speaker distribution in IEMOCAP experiments.

Read the abstract

Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) refers to the recognition of human emotions from natural speech. If done accurately, it can offer a number of benefits in building human-centered context-aware intelligent systems. Existing SER approaches are largely centralized, without considering users' privacy. Federated Learning (FL) is a distributed machine learning paradigm dealing with decentralization of privacy-sensitive personal data. In this paper, we present a privacy-preserving and data-efficient SER approach by utilizing the concept of FL. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first federated SER approach, which utilizes self-training learning in conjunction with federated learning to exploit both labeled and unlabeled on-device data. Our experimental evaluations on the IEMOCAP dataset shows that our federated approach can learn generalizable SER models even under low availability of data labels and highly non-i.i.d. distributions. We show that our approach with as few as 10% labeled data, on average, can improve the recognition rate by 8.67% compared to the fully-supervised federated counterparts.

The approach

How it works

  1. 01

    Attend to speech features

    A convolutional network processes log-mel spectrograms. Spectral, temporal, and channel attention weight features before the model predicts an emotion.

  2. 02

    Use unlabeled speech

    Local self-training combines known labels with pseudo-labels from high-confidence predictions. A device-specific confidence schedule accounts for training progress and each client’s participation.

  3. 03

    Aggregate across devices

    FedAvg combines the locally trained parameters into a shared model. Raw recordings remain on devices, and the method does not require a labeled or unlabeled server dataset.

From the paper

Key findings

Scarce labels still help

On the improvised IEMOCAP subset with four emotion classes, self-training improves unweighted accuracy in all four tested partitions when only 10% of examples are labeled. The comparison uses identical labeled subsets in supervised federated baselines.

Speaker separation is harder

Experiments distinguish speaker-independent evaluation from overlapping speakers, and random partitioning from per-speaker non-IID data. Recognition remains harder when test speakers are unseen, underscoring the importance of the evaluation split.
