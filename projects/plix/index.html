Plug-and-Play Multilingual Few-shot Spoken Words Recognition

Recognize a new spoken word from a few examples

Aaqib Saeed · Vasileios Tsouvalas

At a glance

A new voice command should need examples, not a new training run. PLiX learns an acoustic embedding across languages, then recognizes unseen words by comparing a query with prototypes built from a few labeled support clips.

What this adds

  • 01

    Multilingual episodic training learns to compare words across 20 languages.

  • 02

    Support-derived prototypes let a frozen encoder recognize a new vocabulary without retraining.

  • 03

Abstract

As technology advances and digital devices become prevalent, seamless human-machine communication is increasingly gaining significance. The growing adoption of mobile, wearable, and other Internet of Things (IoT) devices has changed how we interact with these smart devices, making accurate spoken words recognition a crucial component for effective interaction. However, building robust spoken words detection system that can handle novel keywords remains challenging, especially for low-resource languages with limited training data. Here, we propose PLiX, a multilingual and plug-and-play keyword spotting system that leverages few-shot learning to harness massive real-world data and enable the recognition of unseen spoken words at test-time. Our few-shot deep models are learned with millions of one-second audio clips across 20 languages, achieving state-of-the-art performance while being highly efficient. Extensive evaluations show that PLiX can generalize to novel spoken words given as few as just one support example and performs well on unseen languages out of the box. We release models and inference code to serve as a foundation for future research and voice-enabled user interface development for emerging devices.

How it works

PLiX

01

Learn a reusable acoustic space

The multilingual base uses EfficientNet-v2 Medium, trained in 10-way, 5-shot episodes across 20 languages. Training and test vocabularies contain disjoint words.

§2.2–3.3. The TinyNet-E alternatives are separately trained language-specific models, not a compressed copy of the multilingual base.

02

Average support embeddings only

For a requested word c, encode each labeled support clip and average its embedding. The query is excluded from this computation.

pc=1|Sc|∑(x,y)∈Scfθ(x)

§2.1.2, p. 3; Figure 1 explicitly separates the query path from mean pooling.

03

Classify without a weight update

Choose the prototype closest to the query embedding in squared Euclidean distance. Changing support clips changes the classifier while leaving encoder weights fixed.

y^=argminc‖fθ(x)−pc‖22

§2.1.2, expressed equivalently to the paper’s argmax of negative squared distance. Most word tests use one-second clips.

04

Separate recognition from encoder cost

Recognition tests vary vocabulary size, support count and language. Hardware profiling measures encoder inference only on Raspberry Pi 4, Jetson Nano and Pixel 7.

Takeaways

  • A few examples can define a new vocabulary without retraining the encoder; recognition depends on the language, support count and number of competing words.
  • Transfer is bounded: five unseen languages and FLEURS episodes show promise, with harder settings still visible.
  • Lower encoder cost is useful evidence, but it does not measure full application energy or accuracy after quantization.
Study scope & sources
  • This page describes arXiv:2305.03058v1, a 2023 preprint. The implementation source is aaqibsaeed/plixkws on Hugging Face.
  • Evaluation uses segmented, closed-set episodes. Continuous listening, unknown-word rejection and false-trigger rates are not evaluated.
  • FLEURS contains 102 languages, but reported episodes distinguish 3, 5 or 10 languages.
Data
MSWC: over 12 million one-second clips; 16,665 training and 4,174 test keywords across 20 languages, with disjoint keyword splits.
Models
EfficientNet-v2 Medium (52.8M parameters) for the base model; TinyNet-E (761,396 parameters) for language-specific small models; ImageNet initialization.
Transfer
Five unseen languages: Frisian, Mongolian, Maltese, Slovenian and Tamil; separate few-shot language-identification episodes on FLEURS.
Protocol
1,000 test episodes, normally 15 queries; number of classes and support examples varies. Hardware profiling uses Raspberry Pi 4, Jetson Nano and Pixel 7.

Scope & limitations

  • Recognition results are closed-set episodes over segmented clips; open-set rejection, false-trigger rates and continuous streaming keyword detection are not evaluated.
  • Generalization varies by language, vocabulary size and support count; the unseen-language experiment covers five languages, not all languages.
  • Hardware measurements exclude preprocessing and prototype computation, and the paper does not report a corresponding quantized-accuracy comparison.
  • Recognition accuracy is presented graphically without uncertainty intervals; the small models are trained separately per language and are not the single multilingual base model.
