Communication-Efficient Federated Learning through Adaptive Weight Clustering and Server-Side Distillation

Compress both directions of federated training.

Vasileios Tsouvalas · Aaqib Saeed · Tanir Ozcelebi · Nirvana Meratnia

The idea

At a glance

Compressing client updates alone leaves the aggregated server model expensive to send back. FedCompress clusters weights during local training and restores a compact global model through server-side distillation, using unlabeled data and a representation-quality signal to adapt compression as learning progresses without changing the underlying aggregation rule.

  • Federated learning
  • Weight clustering
  • Knowledge distillation
Read the abstract

Federated Learning (FL) is a promising technique for the collaborative training of deep neural networks across multiple devices while preserving data privacy. Despite its potential benefits, FL is hindered by excessive communication costs due to repeated server-client communication during training. To address this challenge, model compression techniques, such as sparsification and weight clustering are applied, which often require modifying the underlying model aggregation schemes or involve cumbersome hyperparameter tuning, with the latter not only adjusts the model's compression rate but also limits model's potential for continuous improvement over growing data. In this paper, we propose FedCompress, a novel approach that combines dynamic weight clustering and server-side knowledge distillation to reduce communication costs while learning highly generalizable models. Through a comprehensive evaluation on diverse public datasets, we demonstrate the efficacy of our approach compared to baselines in terms of communication costs and inference speed.

The approach

How it works

  1. 01

    Cluster weights during local training

    Train client models while encouraging weights to share a small set of learnable centroids. Send the resulting compressed models for standard federated aggregation.

  2. 02

    Compress the aggregated model again

    Use the aggregated global model as a teacher. Train a clustered student to match its predictions on unlabeled, out-of-distribution data held by the server.

  3. 03

    Adjust compression as learning progresses

    Clients estimate representation quality from the effective rank of local embeddings. The server increases the cluster count when this signal plateaus, balancing model capacity and communication.

Key findings

Savings across five vision and audio datasets

Table 1 reports communication reductions of 3.80× to 5.41× relative to FedAvg, with test accuracy 0.31 to 1.88 percentage points lower. The evaluation includes CIFAR-10/100, PathMNIST, SpeechCommands and VoxForge.

Measured inference gains on edge devices

For ResNet-20 and MobileNet on Pixel 6, Jetson Nano and Coral TPU, Table 2 reports approximately 1.10× to 1.15× faster float32 inference than FedAvg models. Results depend on the model, device and numeric format.
